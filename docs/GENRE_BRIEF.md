# What players expect from a game like this

A research brief (2026-11-05) on the games Game of Crowns is measured against: Crusader Kings III, Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord, Kingdom Come: Deliverance, Manor Lords, Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld, The Sims, Shadow of Mordor's Nemesis system, Stronghold, Medieval Dynasty, Kenshi and Skyrim. It guides the play rounds in [PLAY_LEDGER.md](PLAY_LEDGER.md). Figures from fan wikis and forums are indicative, not exact.

## The lessons that shape Aldmere

- **Cheap far, rich near (KCD).** Simulate people far from the camera as a schedule (where they are, what they're doing); build the full agent only near the King. KCD ran about 600 NPCs with about 50 in full detail.
- **Smart objects (The Sims).** Places and objects advertise the need they meet; a person scores the options by need, personality and distance, then picks among the best few, never always the top. Cheap, readable and natural for work and rest; the local model stays for talk.
- **Few, strong memories (Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld, CK3, Generative Agents).** A small set of memories per person, ranked by how strongly they were felt, plus opinions of named people; feed those as context, not the whole history. Believability needs memory, reflection into beliefs, and plans.
- **A memory the player never sees does nothing (Nemesis).** When the King comes back, people mention the past to his face.
- **Crime has witnesses (Skyrim, KCD).** Witnesses flee or report; reputation per town and per class changes greetings, prices and guards; it builds slowly and falls at once.
- **Fence violent goals (Oblivion's Radiant AI).** Unbounded goals made NPCs murder each other; keep hard limits.
- **A storyteller paces the realm (RimWorld, after Left 4 Dead).** Tension builds, then breathing room; size threats by the realm's state; never repeat a beat in a session (CK3's event spam is the warning).
- **Consequences stay on the map (Kenshi).** A fallen lord changes who holds the towns, visibly.
- **One clear lever, a visible answer in seconds (Stronghold).** Every ruling the King makes in his own words leaves at least one visible change in the world and one memory in someone.
- **Walking mode basics (Bannerlord, Manor Lords).** WASD and run, one interact key with a context prompt, heads turn and people greet you, hits with impact (a short freeze, a stagger, a sound), victims flee or fight, bystanders scatter, guards come, mounting is clean, and getting back up to the realm is instant and always works.
- **Tooltips that explain why (CK3).** Nested tooltips and an encyclopedia did more teaching than the tutorial.
- **The first ten minutes:** a sweep over the realm; within a minute a petition answered in your own words with a visible result; within five, a nudge to walk among the people, where someone mentions that ruling; one rivalry already under way. Loading screens carry lore, house words and "previously" lines from the chronicle.

## What players notice when it's missing (by impact)

1. People visibly doing their jobs, not standing idle.
2. People remembering and mentioning what the King did to them, to his face.
3. Crime with consequences: witnesses flee or report, guards respond, the town's view of you changes.
4. Every ruling visibly changes the map or the town within moments.
5. Tooltips that explain why a number changed.
6. Pause and speed, always there and obvious.
7. Day and night routines (markets close, people go home and sleep).
8. A chronicle or notices telling you what happened while you weren't looking.
9. Events paced by tension: no spam, no repeats.
10. Greetings and remarks as you pass, shaped by reputation.
11. Combat with impact; foes react, flee or call for help.
12. A smooth zoom from the realm map to the street.
13. Readable personalities that steer people's choices.
14. Lasting ambition: rivals and crises that keep coming late in a reign.
15. A reliable way back up to the realm, and a mount that works.
